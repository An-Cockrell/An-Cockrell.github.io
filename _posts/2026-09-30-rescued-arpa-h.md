[Dr. Gary An](https://www.medicaldigitaltwins.ai/people.html#gary-an) has received a research and development contract worth up to $38 million from the Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health (ARPA-H), the largest research award in the history of the University of Vermont. The project, **ReSCUED** (Reprogramming Severe Critical Illness Using Extensible Digital Twins), is part of ARPA-H's [CIRCLE program](https://arpa-h.gov/explore-funding/programs/circle).

Over up to five years, ReSCUED will combine frequent bedside measurement of a patient's immune state, a mechanism-based digital twin of that patient, and an AI "virtual consultant" trained on the twin to identify and test treatment strategies with FDA-approved agents before they are given. The An-Cockrell Lab leads the effort and is building the computational models and digital twin platform, working with clinical sites at Wake Forest, UAB (through the Quantum Leap Healthcare Collaborative) and Washington University in St. Louis, and with technology partners DNA Medicine Institute and InflammaSense.

The project is expected to create new computational modeling positions at the Larner College of Medicine.
